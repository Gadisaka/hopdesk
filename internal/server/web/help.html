Guide

How to use HopDesk

HopDesk lets you see another computer's screen and use it with your own mouse and keyboard, from anywhere. For example, you can use your home computer from your phone or from a laptop at work. You don't need any technical knowledge: this page explains every step and every word.

First time?Set up the computer you want to reach. About 5 minutes. Already set up?Connect to it from a browser. Saw a word you don't know?Every term, in plain language.

The three parts

HopDesk has three parts. A phone call is a good comparison: two people (your device and the other computer) and a phone network that connects them (the server).

The server only introduces them. After that, the picture goes straight from one computer to the other.

Agent

“the computer you reach”

This is the HopDesk app, installed on a computer you want to use from somewhere else. It shares that computer's screen and follows the mouse and keyboard you send it.

Lives on
The computer at home or at the office (Mac or Windows)
You need
To install the HopDesk app once

Viewer

“where you sit”

This is a web page you open in any browser (Chrome, Safari, Edge…). It shows the other computer's screen, and your clicks and typing go to that computer.

Lives on
Any phone, tablet or computer with a browser
You need
Nothing to install. Just the address.

Server

“the meeting point”

The server helps the viewer and the agent find each other, like a phone network that connects a call. It also serves the viewer page. It never sees your screen or your password.

Lives on
Usually one of your own computers, by ticking This computer is the server in the app
You need
One per household or team

One computer can be two things at once. If you tick “This computer is the server”, that computer is the meeting point and an agent you can reach. Most people only ever need this one computer.

Who controls whom

This is the question people ask most. The short answer: the browser controls the computer that has the HopDesk app. Never the other way around.

If you want to…Then…
Use your home computer from your phoneInstall HopDesk on the home computer. On the phone, just open the address in a browser.
Use your office PC from your home MacInstall HopDesk on the office PC. On the Mac, open the address in a browser.
Control both ways (A controls B, and B controls A)Install HopDesk on both. Each one can then also open the other in its browser.
Help a family member with their computerInstall HopDesk on their computer and connect it to your server. You open it in your browser.

Installing HopDesk on a computer does not let it control anything else. It only lets you reach that computer, and only with its password.

Set it up, step by step

Pick the situation that matches yours.

Start here if this is your first time. You'll set up one computer that you can reach from anywhere.

  1. Install HopDesk on the computer you want to reach

    Download it from the releases page: the .dmg file for a Mac, the .exe file for Windows. Open it and install like any other app. The HopDesk settings page opens on its own, and a small HopDesk icon appears in the menu bar (Mac) or the system tray (Windows).

  2. Save the password

    A blue box shows this computer's password, for example k7mp-2xqa-9rtw-hd3f. Click Copy and keep it somewhere safe, like a password manager. HopDesk shows it only once. If you lose it, click Generate new to get a new one.

  3. Allow HopDesk on a Mac (skip on Windows)

    Under macOS permissions, click Grant next to each item, then switch on HopDesk in the window that opens:

    • Screen Recording: lets HopDesk send a picture of the screen.
    • Accessibility: lets your mouse and keyboard work.

    After turning on Screen Recording, click Restart HopDesk.

  4. Choose “This computer is the server”

    In the Connection box, click This computer is the server, then click Save and restart at the bottom.

  5. Sign in to Tailscale once

    Click Sign in to Tailscale and create a free account (you can sign in with Google, Microsoft or Apple). Tailscale is built into HopDesk; it's what lets your computer be reached from anywhere without router settings. If the page asks for one more step (turning on HTTPS or Funnel), click the link it shows and press the button there.

  6. Copy the address

    When the green message Reachable from anywhere appears, copy the address next to Open in any browser. It looks like https://hopdesk-studio-mac.tail4b2c.ts.net. Save it as a bookmark on your phone and laptop. That's it: now connect.

Already have one computer set up as the server? Add as many more as you like. They all use that same server.

  1. On the server computer, copy two things

    Open its HopDesk settings. Under Connection, copy:

    • the Other computers' server address (starts with wss://)
    • the Agent key (a long secret code, so strangers can't add their computers to your server)
  2. Install HopDesk on the new computer

    Same as before. Save its password, and on a Mac grant the two permissions.

  3. Choose “Use a HopDesk server”

    Paste the server address into Server address and the agent key into Agent key. Click Save and restart.

  4. Check it says Online

    The badge at the top of the settings page turns green. Now you can reach this computer from the same browser address as the server, by typing its device name and password.

Connect to a computer

Do this from any phone, tablet or computer.

  1. Open the address in a browser

    Use the address you copied (the one starting with https://). You'll see Connect to a device.

  2. Type the device name and password

    Device is the name shown at the top of HopDesk on that computer, for example studio-mac. Password is the one you saved. Click Connect.

  3. The first time: compare the fingerprint

    A box shows a code like 0C36 86F9 7448 8E47…. Check that it's the same as the Fingerprint in HopDesk on that computer, then click It matches, connect. This makes sure you're talking to your real computer and not an impostor. Your browser remembers it, so you only do this once per browser.

  4. Next time, it's quicker

    The computer appears under Your devices, with a green dot when it's online. Tap it and enter the password, or let your phone's or browser's password manager fill it in. To give it a friendlier name or remove it, tap ⋯ on its card. On a phone, add HopDesk to your home screen so it opens like an app.

  5. You're in

    The other screen appears. Click on it, and your mouse and keyboard now work on that computer.

While you're connected

These controls appear at the top of the viewer.

DisplayIf the other computer has more than one screen, pick which one to see.
QualityHow sharp the picture is. Lower it if things feel slow; raise it on fast internet.
fpsHow smooth movement looks. 30 is a good default; 60 is smoother but uses more internet.
Cmd → CtrlUsing a Mac to control a Windows PC? Turn this on so Cmd+C works as Ctrl+C.
SoundThe speaker button. Tap it to hear the other computer; tap again to mute. Sound starts off each time until you've turned it on once. Volume sets how loud it is.
FilesBrowse the other computer's files. Download them to this device, or upload files to it.
DisconnectEnds the session. The other computer keeps running as before.

Keyboard opens your phone's keyboard, plus a row of keys phones don't have: Esc, Tab, Ctrl, Alt, Cmd (or Win), Shift, the arrows and F1–F12. For a shortcut like Ctrl+C, tap Ctrl and then type c. The ? button shows these tips again.

Rotate makes the picture bigger: tap it and turn your phone sideways, and the computer's screen fills your whole display. It works even with your phone's rotation lock on. Tap Upright to get the menu bar back. When you open the keyboard, the page stands up while you type and turns back afterwards.

Copy and paste works between the two computers: copy text on one, paste on the other. (The computer's owner can turn this off.)

Every word explained

Every term you'll see in HopDesk, in alphabetical order. Type to search.

AccessibilityMac permission
A Mac privacy setting. Turning it on for HopDesk lets the mouse and keyboard from your browser work on that Mac. Without it you can watch, but not click or type.Where: settings page → macOS permissions → Grant
Agentthe computer you reach
The HopDesk app on a computer you want to use remotely. It shares that computer's screen and follows your mouse and keyboard. Every computer you install HopDesk on is an agent.See the three parts
Agent key
A long secret code that your computers use to join your server. It stops strangers from adding their computers to it. It's not the same as the password: the password lets a person control a computer, while the agent key lets a computer join the server. You only need it when adding more computers.Where: settings page → Connection
Approve each connection
When this is on, someone sitting at the computer has to agree before anyone can connect, even with the right password: a box pops up asking to Allow (Mac) or answer Yes (Windows) within 30 seconds. Good for a family member's computer, so they always know when you're connecting.Where: settings page → Preferences
Clipboard
Whatever you last copied (with Copy or Ctrl/Cmd+C). HopDesk can share it between the two computers: Sync both ways, Only paste into this computer, or Off.Where: settings page → Preferences
Cmd → Ctrl
Macs use the Cmd key for shortcuts and Windows uses Ctrl. Turn this switch on when you're on a Mac controlling a Windows PC, so your usual shortcuts work.Where: viewer, top bar while connected
Device identity changed
A red warning that the computer is answering with a different fingerprint than last time. That's normal only if you reinstalled HopDesk on it. If you didn't, click Disconnect: someone may be pretending to be your computer. Your password is not sent until you decide.
Device name
The name of a computer in HopDesk, like a contact name, for example studio-mac. You type it in the viewer to choose which computer to connect to. Letters, numbers, dashes, dots and underscores only.Where: settings page → This computer
Display
One screen (monitor) of the other computer. If it has two, choose which one to see.Where: viewer, top bar while connected
End-to-end encrypted
Everything between your browser and the other computer is scrambled so that only those two can read it. Not the server, not your internet provider, not Tailscale.
Files
A panel in the viewer for browsing the other computer's folders. Click a file to download it to the device you're on, or click Upload to send files to the other computer.
Fingerprint
A code that belongs to one specific computer, like a signature, for example 0C36 86F9 7448 8E47 BF9B 6AAB 5B7F 69FB. The first time you connect, the viewer shows it so you can check it's the same as in that computer's HopDesk. After that the browser remembers it and warns you in red if it ever changes. You never need to type it; you only compare it.Where: settings page → This computer
Frame rate (fps)
“Frames per second”: how many pictures of the screen are sent each second. More is smoother but uses more internet. 30 is a good default.
Local address
An advanced setting: the internal address the server uses on the computer itself. Leave it as it is unless someone who set up your network asks you to change it.Where: settings page → Connection (server mode)
Online / Setup needed / Offline
The badge at the top of the settings page. Online (green): ready to be reached. Setup needed (orange): finish the steps on the page. Offline or Error (red): it can't reach the server; the message on the page says why.
Password
The secret that lets a person control this computer. HopDesk makes a strong one for you (like k7mp-2xqa-9rtw-hd3f) and shows it only once, because it keeps only a scrambled version that can't be turned back. Click Choose my own… to use your own (10+ characters) or Generate new for a fresh one.Where: settings page → This computer
Permissions
On a Mac, apps need your OK before they can see the screen or move the mouse. HopDesk asks for two: Screen Recording and Accessibility. Windows doesn't need this.
Quality
How sharp the picture is. Low (1280 pixels wide) works on slow connections, Medium (1920), High (2560, the default) and Ultra (the screen's full resolution). You can change it during a session.
Relay
Sometimes two networks can't talk directly (some office or hotel Wi-Fi blocks it). Then the data is passed along through the server instead. It's still end-to-end encrypted; it may just be a little slower.
Screen RecordingMac permission
A Mac privacy setting. Turning it on for HopDesk lets it send a picture of the screen to your browser. Without it you'd see a black or empty screen. macOS needs HopDesk to restart afterwards.Where: settings page → macOS permissions → Grant
Server
The meeting point that introduces your browser and your computers to each other, like a phone network connecting a call. It also serves the viewer page. You get one by ticking This computer is the server on one of your computers.See the three parts
Server address
Where your other computers find the server. It starts with wss:// and ends with /ws. Copy it from the server computer (Other computers' server) and paste it on each new computer.Where: settings page → Connection
Server mode / Use a HopDesk server
The two choices under Connection. This computer is the server: this computer is the meeting point (and can still be reached). Use a HopDesk server: this computer joins a server you set up somewhere else.
Sound
HopDesk can send what the other computer is playing (music, videos, alerts) to your browser. Tap the speaker button to hear it. The computer only captures sound while someone is listening, and the person there can turn it off completely with Share sound. Sound needs the video engine (ffmpeg). On a Mac it uses the same Screen Recording permission as the picture.Where: viewer, speaker button while connected; settings page, Preferences
Start at login
Starts HopDesk automatically when the computer turns on, so you can still reach it after a restart.Where: settings page → Preferences
Tailscale
A free service built into HopDesk that gives your computer a private address that works from anywhere: no router settings, no port forwarding. You only sign in once, on the server computer.
Tailscale Funnel / HTTPS
Two Tailscale switches that let any browser, including your phone, open your HopDesk address. If HopDesk asks you to turn them on, it shows a link that goes straight to the right button.
Theme
Light or dark colors. The three small buttons in the top-right corner: System (match your device), Light or Dark.
Touchpad and direct modephones and tablets
Two ways to use your finger on a phone. Touchpad: drag to move a pointer and tap to click, like a laptop touchpad. Best for precise work. Direct: tap exactly where you want to click. Switch with the button at the bottom of the screen.See While you're connected
Updates
HopDesk checks for a new version once a day and shows Update available at the top of the settings page and in the menu bar. Click Install update: HopDesk downloads it, makes sure it really comes from HopDesk (every release is signed), installs it and restarts. Anyone connected is disconnected for a few seconds. You can turn the daily check off.Where: settings page → Updates
Video engine
How the screen picture is packed for sending. HopDesk uses H.264, the same kind of video as streaming services, which looks sharp and uses little internet. Nothing to change.
Viewer
The web page you control from. Open it in any browser, at the address from Open in any browser.See the three parts
Viewer address“Open in any browser”
The web address of your HopDesk, like https://hopdesk-studio-mac.tail4b2c.ts.net. Bookmark it on every device you'll connect from.Where: settings page → Connection (server mode)
Your devices
The computers you've connected to from this browser, listed on the connect screen with a green dot when they're online. HopDesk remembers only their names, never their passwords. Your password manager can save those safely. Each browser keeps its own list.Where: viewer, connect screen

If something goes wrong

The most common problems, and what to do.

“Device is not online”
  • Check the HopDesk app is running on that computer: its badge should say Online.
  • Check the device name is spelled exactly the same.
  • Is the computer asleep? Wake it up, or turn on Start at login and change its sleep settings.
“Wrong password”

Passwords are case-sensitive and include the dashes. If you lost it, click Generate new on that computer. After several wrong tries the server makes you wait a little before trying again.

The screen is black or empty

On a Mac, HopDesk needs Screen Recording. Grant it, then click Restart HopDesk.

I can see the screen but can't click or type
  • Click once on the picture first, so your keyboard goes to it.
  • On a Mac, grant Accessibility. If it's already on, switch it off and on again in System Settings.
  • On Windows, the dimmed “Do you want to allow this app…” prompts can't be clicked remotely. Someone at the computer has to answer them.
I can't hear anything
  • Tap the speaker button at the top: sound is off until you turn it on.
  • No speaker button? On that computer, open HopDesk's settings and turn on Share sound. The Video and sound line there says whether sound is working.
  • Check the volume slider, your device's volume, and that something is actually playing on the other computer.
  • On a Mac, sound uses Screen Recording. If you just granted it, click Restart HopDesk.
It's slow or choppy

Lower Quality to Medium or Low, or fps to 15. Both can be changed while connected.

“This page has to be opened over HTTPS”

Browsers only allow secure features on addresses that start with https://. Use the address shown next to Open in any browser in the HopDesk app.

Tailscale keeps asking me to do something

The orange message has a link. Open it, sign in with the same account, and press the button it shows (usually to enable HTTPS or Funnel). HopDesk checks again by itself. Nothing to restart.

A red “Device identity changed” warning

If you just reinstalled HopDesk on that computer, click Forget old identity and compare the new fingerprint. If you didn't, click Disconnect. Your password has not been sent.

Staying safe

  1. Treat the password like a house key

    Only give it to people who should be able to use that computer. Change it with Generate new if you're unsure who has it.

  2. Compare the fingerprint the first time

    It takes ten seconds and guarantees you're connected to your own computer.

  3. Never act on a red warning without thinking

    A changed identity you didn't cause means: disconnect.

  4. Nobody legitimate will ask for HopDesk access

    If a stranger on the phone asks you to install HopDesk or read out your password, hang up. HopDesk is for reaching your own computers.

  5. Use “Approve each connection” on shared computers

    Then nobody can connect unless someone at the computer agrees.
